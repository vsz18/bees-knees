About Your Procedure
Research on BMAC + Intraosseous Bioplasty, and what to expect.
What Is BMAC?
Bone Marrow Aspirate Concentrate
BMAC is harvested from your own hip bone (iliac crest) using a needle under local anesthesia — no donor tissue involved. The aspirate is then spun in a centrifuge to concentrate the healing agents 6–8× above baseline.
What's in the concentrate:
◆
Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) — can differentiate into cartilage cells (chondrocytes) and bone cells (osteoblasts)
◆
Growth factors — TGF-β, BMP-2, PDGF, IGF-1, and VEGF stimulate tissue repair and reduce local inflammation
◆
Platelets — release healing signals and initiate the repair cascade at the defect site

The Intraosseous Component
Bioplasty — Targeting the Bone
Standard joint injections deliver BMAC into the joint space. Intraosseous bioplasty goes deeper — the BMAC is injected directly into the subchondral bone, exactly where the bone marrow edema lesion (BML) lives.
◆
Small targeted perforations are made in the bone to release BML pressure and create channels for the BMAC to integrate
◆
The BML is considered the primary pain driver — this approach addresses it directly rather than just the cartilage surface
◆
The combined approach (intra-articular + intraosseous) targets both the Grade IV cartilage defect and the underlying bone pathology simultaneously
What to Expect
Timeline & Recovery
◆
Procedure day: 1–2 hours, outpatient. Go home same day.
◆
Weeks 1–2: Pain may temporarily increase — this is a normal inflammatory healing response.
◆
Weeks 4–8: Protected weight-bearing. Isometrics, pool, gentle range-of-motion work.
◆
Weeks 6–12: First signs of improvement typically emerge. Gradual return to gym loading.
◆
6–12 months: Full effect of the BMAC. Return-to-run protocol begins if healing is confirmed.
Why Prehab Matters
Studies consistently show that pre-operative quadriceps strength is the single strongest predictor of post-operative recovery speed. Every session you complete now directly accelerates the timeline after the procedure.
◆
Stronger quads = better mechanical protection for the joint surface during loading
◆
Better cardiovascular fitness going in → faster healing metabolism post-procedure
◆
Hip strength reduces patellofemoral contact forces by up to 30% during daily movement

Movement Rules
🚫 Always Avoid
🦘
All jumping and impact — box jumps, jump squats, running, plyometrics
🦵
Deep knee flexion past 60–70° under load (deep squats, full lunges, cyclist squat)
🌀
Twisting under load
🔴
Anything causing knee pain above 3/10 — modify or stop
✅ Always OK
🏋️
Hip-dominant work: Romanian deadlifts, hip thrusts, deadlifts, glute bridges
💪
All upper body training as programmed
🚴
Bike, row erg, ski erg at low–moderate effort
🚶
Walking on flat terrain — daily, comfortable pace
